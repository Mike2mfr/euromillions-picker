What the research in your folder says

• Crowds are predictable, the balls aren't. A study of real lotto players (Suetens, Galbo-Jørgensen & Tyran) found people bet less on numbers drawn last week and more on "hot" numbers. Picking the opposite won't help you win, but it lowers the chance of sharing a jackpot.

• Limiting yourself to "lucky" subsets reduces your coverage (Stömmer, 2026). The "Spread tickets" option makes several tickets cover as many different numbers as possible instead.

• Big jackpots sell more tickets, which means more people to share with (Abrams & Garibaldi, 2025). A ticket is always a bad bet on average, but avoiding popular numbers matters most when the jackpot is huge.

• Prediction models (Nkomozake's Dirichlet model, LotoIA's "Hybrid" engine) can't beat a fair, independent draw: the Test button shows "smart" tickets win about as often as any other. Treat any "convergence" or "prediction" score as entertainment.
